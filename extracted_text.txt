 Interactive Simulation of Ecosystem Food Chains Overview: This project simulates an ecosystem consisting of producers, herbivores, and carnivores. It demonstrates how energy flows across trophic levels (plants → herbivores → carnivores) and how predator-prey population dynamics impact ecosystem stability. The aim of the project is to apply object-oriented programming to model living organisms, interactions, and energy transfer in a food chain. Basic knowledge: Food Chain Structure: Producers: Plants that convert sunlight into energy through photosynthesis. Primary Consumers: Herbivores that eat producers to gain energy. Secondary Consumers: Carnivores that eat herbivores to gain energy. Energy Transfer: Only about 10% of the energy is transferred from one trophic level to the next (10% Rule). Population Dynamics: Predator-prey relationships influence growth and decline in populations, creating fluctuations and cycles. Carrying Capacity: Each ecosystem has a maximum sustainable population for each species, based on available resources. Specifications: GUI: The GUI can be freely designed, but the focus is on demonstrating OOP concepts rather than interface aesthetics. For inspiration, you can refer to simulation apps such as Ecosystem Simulator The ecosystem should be displayed as a grid where each cell may contain one organism (plant, herbivore, or carnivore). Main Menu: • Title of the application. • Options to select different simulation scenarios, e.g.: Balanced Ecosystem (stable interactions). Overpopulation (one species grows excessively). Extinction (loss of one species). • A Help menu explaining the simulation rules and concepts. • A Quit button that requires user confirmation before exiting. Simulation Screen: • Grid-based ecosystem view: Producers (plants): stationary, shown as small green icons. They spawn automatically in random empty cells after intervals. Herbivores: medium-sized icons, move to adjacent cells to consume plants. Carnivores: larger predator icons, move to adjacent cells to hunt herbivores. • Movement: Herbivores and carnivores move randomly or toward the nearest food source within range. Movement speed adjustable via settings. • Spawning and reproduction: Producers regenerate over time. Herbivores and carnivores reproduce if their energy exceeds a threshold. New organisms spawn in adjacent empty cells. • Interactions: Producers: generate energy through photosynthesis, consumed by herbivores. Herbivores: consume producers to gain energy, lose energy over time if no food is available, may die if energy runs out. Carnivores: hunt herbivores for energy, lose energy if prey is not available. • Energy transfer: Herbivores gain ~10% of a producer’s energy when consumed. Carnivores gain ~10% of a herbivore’s energy when consumed. • Population &amp; Energy visualization: Real-time graphs or counters showing population size per species. Visualization of energy flow between trophic levels. • Controls: Adjust birth rates, death rates, and energy efficiency. Step through or run simulation continuously. • Always have a Back button to return to the main menu. Help Menu: • Explains the rules of the food chain, energy transfer (10% Rule), and predator-prey population dynamics. Exit Option: • Exits the application after confirmation. Knowledge summary: Food Chain Structure: Producers (Plants): Producers, like plants and algae, are the primary energy sources in an ecosystem. They convert sunlight into chemical energy through photosynthesis , which is then passed on to consumers (herbivores and carnivores). Primary Consumers (Herbivores) Herbivores are organisms that feed on plants and gain energy from them. They play a critical role in transferring energy from producers to higher trophic levels. Secondary Consumers (Carnivores): Carnivores, such as lions or wolves, consume herbivores. They are higher in the food chain and transfer energy to yet higher predators or decomposers. Energy Transfer in Ecosystems: The flow of energy from one trophic level to the next is inefficient, with only about 10% of the energy being passed on to the next level. This phenomenon is known as the 10% Energy Rule , where most energy is lost as heat during metabolic processes. Population Dynamics: Predator-Prey Relationships: The population of predators and prey is interdependent. If the predator population increases, it may reduce the prey population, leading to a decrease in food for predators, and consequently, the predator population also declines. This cycle can lead to fluctuations in population sizes. Carrying Capacity: The carrying capacity of an ecosystem is the maximum population size of a species that the environment can sustain based on available resources (food, shelter, water). If the population exceeds this capacity, it can result in resource depletion and eventual population decline Trophic Levels and Ecological Pyramids: Trophic Levels represent the different levels in a food chain, from producers at the bottom to top predators. Ecological Pyramids visually represent the number of organisms, biomass, or energy at each trophic level, typically showing a decrease in number or energy as you go up the pyramid
